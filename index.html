The problem 02

Repetition can create rhythm.
Golf asks for adaptation.

The range is predictable.
The course isn't.

Most golfers practice in blocks. Ten drivers. Ten seven-irons. Ten wedges.

But golf rarely asks you to hit the same shot twice. Range Lab introduces controlled randomness, so every ball requires a new target, a new decision, and a fresh commitment.

“You don't get the same shot twice on the course. Why practice like you do?”

The method 03

Four steps.
One honest session.

Quick enough for the range. Deliberate enough to change how you practice.

  1. 01

    Build your bag

    Select the clubs you actually carry and add your comfortable stock distances.

    Personal yardages
  2. 02

    Set the session

    Choose Random Practice or play course-like sequences. Pick the number of balls.

    10 · 20 · 30 · 50
  3. 03

    Get the shot

    A club, a target distance, and one clear intention. Choose a real target and commit.

    7 iron · 155 yards
  4. 04

    Record. Reset.

    Log only what you noticed, or skip feedback. Then let the shot go and move on.

    No correction ball

No mulligans. No repeating it until it feels good. Hit it. Log it. Move on.

Inside a session 04

One clear question
at a time.

Range Lab removes the choice of what to hit next. Your only job is to see the shot, choose a target, and make one committed swing.

  • Feedback is always optional
  • Every target uses your carry distances
  • Your session is saved after every shot

Controlled randomness 05

Every club change is a reset.

Switching clubs, distances, and intentions asks you to rebuild the shot from the beginning. That variability is designed to make range practice feel more like the decisions of real golf.
